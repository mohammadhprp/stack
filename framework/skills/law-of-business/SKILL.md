# Law of Business

A pressure-test for a business, product, plan, pivot, price, or fundraise, built from 442 real post-mortems. It states the 21 ways companies actually die as named laws, each with the failure mechanism, the warning signs, the cases that prove it, a counter-move, and a self-check. Use it to find which laws a business violates **before** the market finds them. It is diagnostic, not motivational: it will tell you a thing is broken if it is.

## How to apply

1. **State what is being judged.** Name the subject (an idea, a product, a struggling company, a pivot, a price, a go-to-market plan, a fundraise) and the decision it must inform. A vague subject produces a vague verdict.
2. **Route by symptom.** Match the situation in the References Index below to one or more reference files. Do not answer from memory.
3. **Read every mapped reference in full** before judging. Each law carries its own mechanism and cases; a partial read is how you miss the law that is actually killing the business.
4. **Run `references/diagnostic-checklist.md` for a verdict.** Answer from evidence, mark `yes`/`no`/`unknown`, treat `unknown` as `no`, and apply the scoring rule. A red on **B1. Runway** or **A1. Real Problem** is an automatic stop.
5. **Cite laws by ID and name, with cases.** E.g. "C3. The Law of Proof Before Scale — GroupSpaces scaled paid channels before proving the model." Never assert a failure without a law and a real case behind it.
6. **End with warning signs and counter-moves.** Give the specific, observable signals to watch and the concrete action to take, with a date to re-check.
7. **Refuse generic advice.** Say "this looks fine" only after the checks pass, and say plainly when the honest answer is "do not build this."

## When to use this skill

- **Deciding whether to build.** An idea, product, or business is being evaluated for the first time and the question is "is this worth building?".
- **Diagnosing a struggling company.** Growth has stalled, churn is high, the raise is failing, or the board wants to know why.
- **Pressure-testing a plan, pivot, price, or go-to-market.** Before committing capital, headcount, or a public strategy.
- **Testing a fundraise or a bridge.** Before raising, or before choosing between raising and cutting burn.
- **Reading risk in diligence.** An investor or advisor wants a structured read of what could kill a company.
- **Plain-language triggers:** "is this a good business", "why do startups fail", "what kills companies like mine", "red flags", "should I pivot", "pressure-test my plan", "go/no-go", "unit economics", "runway", "product-market fit", "burn rate", "CAC vs LTV".

## Do not use this skill when

- The task is a legal, tax, or financial-compliance opinion; this skill names regulatory risk as a failure mode, it does not replace counsel.
- The user needs motivating language or a pitch: this skill is built to find flaws, and reading it in a supportive voice defeats it.
- The question is general software engineering or operations unrelated to whether a business will survive (use an engineering skill).
- The user wants a market-size report, a financial model, or due-diligence data collection rather than a judgement against the laws.
